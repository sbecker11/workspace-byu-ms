The file you named is the two-page BYU ScholarsArchive record, not the article. The skills below are from the paper itself: Broekhuijsen, Becker, and Barrett, “Probabilistic Segmentation of Myocardial Tissue by Deterministic Relaxation,” Computers in Cardiology, 1989.

Medical imaging

Cine CT of the heart, using mid-heart end-diastole slices
Myocardial segmentation where only natural contrast separates adjoining tissues
Contrast-enhanced imaging, where intensity thresholding isolates the endocardial surface
Landmark alignment using the spinal canal and the top of the heart wall
CT density normalization: a linear ramp between lung (air) and bone, with clipping
Spatial scale normalization by subsampling, avoiding bilinear interpolation so no new densities appear at tissue boundaries
Segmentation and probabilistic modeling

Adapting Karssemeijer’s deterministic-relaxation organ segmentation from 3D abdominal CT to 2D myocardial CT
Bayes labeling: class probability from the voxel’s intensity given its label, times the probability of that label given its neighbors
Priors from both position and density
Neighborhood interaction: count of neighboring labels, plus a class-to-class boundary interaction table
Six-class labeling: myocardium, blood, bone, interstitial tissue, miscellaneous tissue, and lung
Iterative relaxation: seed with training priors, recompute probabilities, assign each pixel to the maximum class, replace the priors, and stop at convergence
Reducing overly polarized position priors so boundaries can still move on later iterations
System building and evaluation

A four-part pipeline: manual segmentation, training-set construction, prior calculation, and automated segmentation
Manual labeling by painting into an overlay buffer
Incremental learning: fold each new segmentation back into the training set
Static interaction parameters, with the training set left extensible
Testing on abnormal cases, including aneurysms, and judging both accuracy and how many iterations were required
An implementation that runs the segmentation on relatively low-cost hardware
Downstream uses named in the paper

Wall-thickening measurement, myocardial perfusion estimation, and 3D/4D display of the myocardial surface
